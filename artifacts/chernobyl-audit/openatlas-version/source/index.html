01 / 3 MIN

A city built
around power.

The plant was a workplace.
Pripyat was a home.

In 1970, the Soviet Union began building Pripyat to house the workers of a new nuclear power station in northern Ukraine. Its apartments, schools and public spaces expressed confidence in a technological future.

By April 1986, four RBMK units were operating at Chernobyl. Each had an electrical output of about 1,000 megawatts. The enormous turbine halls tell part of the story: a nuclear station is also a steam power station. Fission provides heat; turbines and generators convert some of that heat into electricity.

The exhibit is a composite of the plant’s recognizable forms, not a survey of Unit 4. Its block-like reactor building, long turbine hall and braced ventilation chimney establish the relationship between spaces. Building dimensions, counts of channels and pipe routes are simplified. The intact configuration matters: seeing only ruins can make the accident seem inevitable and erase the ordinary life that preceded it.

02 / 4 MIN

One reactor.
Two different jobs.

Graphite slows neutrons. Water carries heat away. In an RBMK, those jobs are performed by different materials—and that distinction matters.

Fission splits heavy atomic nuclei, releasing energy and more neutrons. A controlled chain reaction maintains a steady rate of fission. The graphite moderator slows neutrons so they are more likely to cause further fission in the fuel. Water flows through pressure tubes containing fuel assemblies, removing heat and partly boiling.

▦

Graphite

The moderator remains in place as the water boils. It is not the fuel.

≈

Water

Coolant removes heat. It also absorbs some neutrons, affecting the chain reaction.

↓

Control rods

Absorbers regulate reactivity. Original rod design created a dangerous insertion effect in some conditions.

Above the core, separator drums divide a steam–water mixture. Steam travels to turbines; separated liquid returns through the circulation system. After leaving the turbine, steam condenses back into water and feedwater pumps return it to the cycle. A separate cooling-water circuit removes waste heat in the condenser. The two water circuits exchange heat, not water, in normal operation.

A steam bubble is a “void” in the liquid coolant. In the RBMK under the accident conditions, increasing voids could increase reactivity: less water meant less neutron absorption while graphite continued moderating. More power could then produce more boiling, producing still more power. This is a positive feedback, not a statement that every operating state inevitably runs away.

More boiling→Less absorption→More fission↺

03 / 3 MIN

26 April 1986.
A failure of layers.

An accident cannot be explained by a single button.

Unit 4 was preparing for a test of whether a coasting turbine could briefly power equipment as emergency diesel generators started. The test was electrical; its consequences became nuclear.

25 APRIL

A delayed shutdown

Power reduction was interrupted by grid demand. The eventual test took place during a changed operating sequence.

EARLY 26 APRIL

An unstable operating state

Power fell unexpectedly. Operators recovered to a low level, with an unsafe combination of core conditions and withdrawn control rods. Instrumentation and procedures did not make the full risk apparent.

01:23

Test, shutdown command, rapid surge

The turbine test began. When the emergency shutdown command was given, the original rod design could initially add positive reactivity in the lower core. The resulting power excursion ruptured fuel channels and destroyed the reactor.

THE FOLLOWING HOURS

An open, burning reactor

Explosions and fires released radioactive material. Firefighters and plant workers responded without a complete understanding of the radiation hazards.

The precise contribution of individual mechanisms in the final seconds is not reducible to this diagram. The IAEA’s INSAG-7 report revised the earlier emphasis on operator error, highlighting reactor design deficiencies, inadequate safety information, regulatory weaknesses and the broader safety culture. Human actions mattered within a system that failed to keep foreseeable mistakes from becoming catastrophic.

This was not a nuclear weapon detonation. A reactor power excursion, fuel damage, pressure-driven destruction and subsequent fires are different physical processes from a bomb. Avoiding that misconception helps us discuss the event accurately without minimizing it.

04 / 3 MIN

The long
aftermath.

Pripyat was evacuated on 27 April, roughly 36 hours after the accident. Residents were told to prepare for a temporary absence. For most, there would be no return home.

Workers often called “liquidators” contained fires, removed contaminated material, constructed the first shelter and carried out a vast cleanup. Acute radiation sickness affected highly exposed responders; later health impacts included a marked increase in thyroid cancer among people exposed as children and adolescents, especially through radioactive iodine. Radiation effects vary by dose, route, age and time. Broad estimates of eventual deaths depend on methods and assumptions and should not be presented as a single settled number.

1986

Emergency shelter

A structure built under extreme conditions to enclose the damaged unit.

2000

Last unit closed

Other reactors continued operating for years. Unit 3 was the last to shut down.

2016

A new enclosure

The New Safe Confinement was moved into position over the old shelter.

Containment is an ongoing engineering responsibility, not an erasure of the accident. Decommissioning, waste management and the protection of workers extend across generations. This notebook’s historical model is not an assessment of the site’s present condition.

The exclusion zone is sometimes photographed as nature “recovering.” Wildlife presence does not prove that radiation is harmless. Reduced human activity, habitat change and radiation exposure operate together. Equally, images of empty buildings cannot communicate the whole social cost: displacement, lost communities, stigma and uncertainty have shaped lives far beyond the site boundary.

A second thought: would better training alone have been enough?

Training helps, but it cannot substitute for safe design, useful instrumentation, independent oversight and honest communication of hazards. INSAG-7 places the accident in this wider system. A robust safety approach prevents errors, limits their consequences and gives people a realistic picture of risk.

A small connection to
the Tauri framework.

This notebook runs entirely in the browser. Tauri is a way to package a web interface like this as a desktop or mobile application, with native capabilities handled by a Rust application core.

Web interface
HTML · CSS · Three.js⇄Controlled commands
Tauri IPC boundary⇄Rust core
Native capabilities

In a Tauri project, a frontend build creates static web assets and Tauri loads them in the platform’s webview. Unlike shipping a full browser with the application, using a system webview can keep distribution size smaller; rendering behavior and available features still need testing on each target platform.

A desktop version might let a learner export notes to a file. The web interface would request a narrowly scoped operation, and the native layer would validate the input and write only where permitted. Tauri 2 capabilities control access to application commands and plugin permissions. A frontend button should not become an unrestricted gateway to the file system.

Inspect a minimal project map
chernobyl-atlas/
  src/                 # editable web interface
  dist/                # built static assets
  src-tauri/
    src/lib.rs         # Rust application entry
    tauri.conf.json    # window + build configuration
    capabilities/     # explicit permissions

A typical build runs the frontend build command, then tauri build. This notebook includes the web source only; it is not a compiled Tauri application. Its offline, bundled assets make a useful starting point.

Try this design question: which part should rotate the camera? Keep that in the frontend. Which part should write an exported lesson file? In a native app, use a constrained native command or permitted file plugin. The boundary is about responsibility and access, not visual appearance.
