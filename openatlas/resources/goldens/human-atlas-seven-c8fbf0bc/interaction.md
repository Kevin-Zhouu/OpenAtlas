# Interaction Analysis

## Primary interaction
Initial: The loaded full body shows 2,229 visible pieces.
Action: Click Skeleton.
Response: Visible count changes to 296 and tissue is replaced by the skeleton (interaction-1.png).
Why it matters: A system can be inspected without surrounding occlusion.

Action: Focus the explosion range and press End.
Response: 100% explosion arranges bone pieces into an anatomical inventory; the footer changes orbit guidance to pan guidance (interaction-2.png).
Action: Assemble and reset.
Response: Full-body geometry and 2,229 visible pieces return (interaction-3.png).

## Secondary interactions
Heart system selection leaves 23 visible pieces at their original locations (interaction-4.png). Side view changes camera orientation (interaction-5.png). These are meaningful visibility and spatial controls. The filtered heart remains small, so selection alone is not sufficient close inspection.
Source & credits opens a panel explaining the model, data licence and limitations (interaction-6.png).

## Loading and responsive observation
The first timed screenshot caught 47% loading. It was replaced after waiting for Preparing the anatomy to disappear. No browser errors were logged in the final live run. At 390×844 the source panel is readable and vertically scrollable; broader mobile model manipulation was not verified.

## Evidence limits
No random clicks were used. Search, direct object picking and isolation exist in the documented source but were not observed here. Naming and visibility support spatial recognition; they do not establish learning of function.
