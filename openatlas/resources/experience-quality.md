EXPERIENCE QUALITY (applies to manual briefs, planned briefs, and every repair):
You own the quality of the learner's experience. Translate the original brief into a short source/EXPERIENCE.md before implementation: primary learning interaction, scene/hierarchy organization, transitions and visual failure cases. Include a short acceptance checklist covering the brief’s major required behaviors, teaching goals and delivery requirements; separate these from optional design polish. The user need not supply acceptance tests. Build and inspect the primary interaction early, before polishing prose. Preserve it through every repair. A passing status-text check is not permission to degrade the experience.

Browser tools: the browser MCP server is preinstalled and available. Use its persistent browser and screenshot tools for exploratory review, with screenshots returned as image content. Screenshot files are automatically named beneath source/review; use the returned paths in your evidence. Saving a PNG is not viewing it. Start a local server in /workspace and inspect the final dist build, including inside an opaque-origin sandbox="allow-scripts" iframe. Use browser actions to drag, scroll, select and change controls. Keep programmatic source tests for measurable invariants. Do not install another browser tool or substitute generated illustrations for working interaction.

For a continuous scroll-driven scene, derive a fractional timeline from scroll position and interpolate the camera, transforms, opacity and relevant clipping values between adjacent states. Keep one master model. A nearest-chapter switch alone does not satisfy continuous motion. Render during scrolling; do not debounce/suspend rendering until scrolling stops or impose a slow fixed animation cadence to satisfy a click timeout. Optimize resolution, detail and per-frame work instead. Inspect normal motion forwards and backwards and intermediate states; reduced-motion testing is a separate accessibility check.

For hierarchical assembly/disassembly, separate the current scope's immediate child GROUPS, with every eligible descendant retaining a shared group transform and its internal spatial relationships. Drill down before showing individual parts. Paginate groups at the current hierarchy level; do not flatten the whole model into the first few leaf meshes and hide the rest as soon as the slider moves. Test membership coverage, assembled coordinates, intermediate separation, spacing, hidden-state persistence and undo. Preserve normalized-scale explanations. These requirements apply when the brief asks for this behavior, not to unrelated lessons.

Review the actual final build as a learner. Inspect overview, intermediate motion, focused/deep content, demanding separated/cut state, and phone layout. Record exact screenshots viewed and observed defects in source/EXPERIENCE.md. Explain resource limitations without silently weakening the brief. An independent reviewer will inspect the final artifact against the original request, not just your manifest or self-report. Fix discrepancies before finishing.

For real-time hierarchical 3D work, tested starter functions are available in /opt/openatlas-runtime/scene-state.mjs (with tests). Copy them into source and adapt the data interface, or implement equivalent invariants. They provide continuous timeline blending, child-group coverage checks, shared group transforms, bounded layouts, pagination and persistent visibility. They contain no anatomy: obtain actual licensed source meshes. Keep controls and prose appropriate to the requested experience.

Repair efficiency: fix all reported blockers together. Preserve working design decisions and completed evidence. Reproduce the reported sequences, test affected behavior, and run a short final regression sweep. Optional reviewer suggestions do not require redesign. Reuse installed dependencies and browser tools; do not restart implementation or rerun unrelated long tests merely because a repair turn began.
